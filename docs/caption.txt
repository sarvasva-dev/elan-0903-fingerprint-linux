"Hardware not supported on Linux" — the 5 words every Linux enthusiast hates hearing. 🐧

When I installed Linux on my ASUS Vivobook 16, the ElanTech fingerprint sensor (04F3:0903) was completely dead. Upstream fprintd failed 100% of the time because the legacy Bozorth algorithm couldn't handle small capacitive frames.

Instead of accepting it and typing my password every 5 minutes, I decided to reverse-engineer the sensor and build our own custom standalone biometric Linux driver from scratch. 🛠️

Here is how the engineering pipeline works under the hood 👇

---

🔍 THE ROOT CAUSE:
Upstream libfprint relies on Bozorth3 — a minutiae algorithm designed decades ago for 500 DPI optical FBI flatbed scanners.
Modern laptop sensors are tiny capacitive grids (80x80 raw). Bozorth demands at least 10 minutiae to attempt a match; on this compact sensor, it only extracted 2, aborting with Score 0 every time.

⚡ THE REVERSE-ENGINEERED ENGINE:

1️⃣ USB Protocol Reverse Engineering:
Decompiled the official Windows driver binaries (WbfUsbDriver.dll) to map out exact USB vendor control transfers and bulk streaming endpoints:
• 0x01 Bulk OUT (Command Interface)
• 0x83 Bulk IN (Touch Interrupt Detection)
• 0x82 Bulk IN (16-bit Raw Capacitive Image Stream)

2️⃣ Signal Enhancement via Computer Vision:
Raw capacitive scans suffer from high fixed-pattern noise (FPN). I engineered an OpenCV pipeline:
• Dynamic background frame subtraction to isolate live ridge signals.
• Percentile contrast clipping (2%–98%).
• Contrast Limited Adaptive Histogram Equalization (CLAHE) for ridge-valley clarity.
• 2x Bicubic upscaling to transform the 80x80 sensor grid into a rich 160x160 biometric frame.

3️⃣ Minutiae & Inlier Engine (ORB + RANSAC):
While Bozorth only found 2 points, our CV pipeline extracts 189 distinct keypoints per touch!
Using k-NN matching and affine homography verification, our security threshold requires 6 inliers — our average touch scores a confident 17 to 25.

4️⃣ Deep Linux PAM & Polkit Integration:
Integrated directly into the Linux PAM stack (/etc/pam.d/common-auth):
• Inter-process mutex locking (fcntl) to eliminate multi-worker race conditions on GDM lock screen.
• Sandboxed /opt deployment so Polkit daemons (with ProtectHome=yes) can authenticate graphical privilege prompts seamlessly.
• Instant passwordless `sudo` and lock screen unlock in under 300ms!

5️⃣ Native GTK 4 / Libadwaita App:
Built a native GNOME management app with Libadwaita so users can enroll multiple fingers (Thumb, Index, etc.) and delete them with one click.

---

📊 RESULTS:
⚡ <300ms authentication latency
⚡ Confident Match Score of 25 (threshold: 6)
⚡ Seamless passwordless sudo, GDM lock screen, and Polkit GUI popups

I've fully open-sourced this driver for the Linux community. If you have an ASUS Vivobook or any laptop with an Elan 04F3:0903 sensor, you can now enjoy 100% working biometric login on Linux!
